Vertical short dramas went from a niche curiosity to a category with dozens of competing apps in about two years. Most write-ups about them are app-store screenshots and affiliate buttons. What almost nobody publishes is the boring-but-useful part: a clean table you can sort, filter and cite. So we made one.

We track the apps people actually search for — DramaBox, ReelShort, ShortMax, NetShort, GoodShort and the rest of the pack — and record the handful of fields that decide which one a viewer should install: how it's rated in the stores, what a week of coins or a subscription really costs, whether it works on a given platform, and whether it's even available in a user's country. The numbers move, so we refresh them every month and keep the previous snapshots.

What's in the dataset

Each row is one app. Each monthly snapshot is a separate file, so you can compare how pricing and ratings drift over time. The fields:

Fields in short-drama-apps-2026.csv / .json
FieldMeaning
appApp name (e.g. DramaBox)
ios_rating / play_ratingAverage store rating at snapshot date
ios_reviews / play_reviewsNumber of ratings behind that average
weekly_price_usdCost of the main weekly plan, in USD
has_free_episodesWhether the app offers genuinely free episodes
platformsiOS, Android, Web, TV
snapshot_dateDate the values were read from the stores
The numeric values in the current files are populated from the ShortDramaTop monthly report. Treat the report as the authoritative version; this copy exists so the data is easy to download and reuse under an open licence.

How the data is collected

Ratings and review counts come straight from the public Apple App Store and Google Play listings, read one app at a time on the snapshot date. Prices are taken from each app's in-app purchase screen and converted to USD where needed. Availability is checked per store front. We don't install-and-guess: every figure traces back to a store listing or an official help page, and the date is recorded alongside it.

What we deliberately leave out is anything we can't verify — invented user counts, "tested by our team" claims without receipts, or star ratings we made up. If a value isn't confirmable, it's blank, not fabricated. The scoring model that turns these raw numbers into rankings is documented on the main site, so you can disagree with the weighting and still trust the inputs.

Using and citing it

The dataset is released under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0. You're free to copy it, chart it, or build on it — including commercially — as long as you credit the source. The required attribution is a visible line with a link: “Data: ShortDramaTop, shortdramatop.com”.
